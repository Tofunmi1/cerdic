# Handoff: procedural water waves (ADR 0070 port 3, the lake of RedGPU without its bitmaps)

Reference (read first, read-only, MIT): `engine/third_party/redgpu/src/water/lakeCore/shader/lakeFragment.wgsl` (30 KB), `lakeVertex.wgsl`, `LakeMaterial.ts`, `interaction/LakeWaveSimulator.ts` and `lakeWaveSimulation.wgsl`. The lake reads two bitmap normal maps (`normalTexture`, `normalDetailTexture`) for its waves and caustics; we have no bitmaps and ship none: waves must come from maths. Our water today is texture-free already (colour by depth, a flowing ripple, foam carried on the mesh as vertex colour; `crates/ve-render/src/surface_water.rs`, `ProceduralRecipe::Water`, the recipe-10 vertex wobble in `crates/ve-render/src/shaders/forward.wgsl` and its fragment part). This job replaces the two sines and the flat ripple with a real wave field.

## Part 1: the wave field (CPU reference, `crates/ve-render/src/water_waves.rs`)
Public API the test uses: `GRAVITY` (9.81), `WAVES` (a const in 8..=16), `Wave { direction_deg, wavelength, amplitude, phase }` (Copy, Debug), `wave_table(seed) -> [Wave; WAVES]`, `WaveField::new(seed: u32, ripple: f32, flow: f32, direction_deg: f32)`, `WaveField::from_waves(&[Wave], flow)`, `.waves() -> &[Wave]`, `.height(x, z, t)`, `.normal(x, z, t) -> [f32; 3]`, `.max_height()`.
Maths: `height = sum_i A_i * sin(k_i * (d_i . p) - w_i * flow * t + phase_i)`, `k = 2 pi / wavelength`, deep-water dispersion `w = sqrt(GRAVITY * k)`, `d_i` from `direction_deg`. The table is a deterministic spread of sizes (shortest to longest ratio above 8; 0.3 to 12 m), directions within 70 degrees of the main direction, amplitudes that fall with shorter wavelength so the steepest slope at `ripple` 1 lies between 0.1 and 0.9 and the largest height stays under 0.15 m. `ripple` scales amplitudes linearly and is clamped to 0..2, `flow` clamped to 0..4, non-finite inputs become the defaults (ripple 1, flow 1, direction 0). `normal` is the analytic gradient, unit length. Document public items; no unwrap/expect/panic.

## Part 2: the shader (`crates/ve-render/src/shaders/water_waves.wgsl`, included into the forward shader the way `wind.wgsl` is)
- `const WAVE_COUNT: u32 = <WAVES>u;` and `fn water_wave_height(p: vec2<f32>, t: f32) -> f32`, `fn water_wave_normal(p: vec2<f32>, t: f32) -> vec3<f32>`, looping over the wave table. The table is built on the CPU by `wave_table`/`WaveField` and travels in a uniform (find how `wind` travels in the view uniform and where `SurfaceWater` parameters reach the material; put the table where it costs one small uniform per water material, or per view if water materials share one). The shader does not recompute the table: parity with the CPU comes from sharing the numbers.
- Vertex: replace the two sines of recipe 10 with `water_wave_height` (displacement only where the normal faces up, as now). Fragment (the water recipe): use `water_wave_normal` for shading instead of the current ripple, add a second detail layer of small procedural ripples (a few high-frequency waves of the same function with a different seed folded in, strength `ripple`), a Fresnel term (reflection of the sky at grazing angles, mix with the existing depth colour; use the sky/ambient the forward shader already samples), a sun glint from the existing sun direction and specular path, and foam breakup (the carried foam value modulated by a cheap procedural noise so the edge is not a smooth gradient). Colour by depth, flow direction from the vertex colour and the `SurfaceWater` fields (`shallow`, `deep`, `clarity`, `flow`, `ripple`, `foam`) keep their meaning; defaults must look at least as good as now. No textures, no new bindings beyond the table.
- WebGL2 must keep working: no compute, no storage textures. Add the shader to the shader validation (`crates/ve-render/src/shader_tests.rs` checks that every shader parses, validates and translates to GLSL ES 3.00); keep that test passing.
- Counters/size: the forward shader must stay valid for every recipe; water code runs only in the water branch.
